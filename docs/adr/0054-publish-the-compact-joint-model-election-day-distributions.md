# Publish the compact joint model's election-day distributions

The published mayoral forecast is produced by the compact joint model (`backend/model/compact_mayoral`), replacing the lightweight poll-average forecast of `backend-2026-09-21.3` and superseding ADRs 0049 and 0053 for the mayoral feed. The model is a hierarchical state-space model fitted jointly over the seven historical Toronto mayoral campaigns (2003–2023) and the current one: named-candidate support as log-odds contrasts on a Gaussian random walk at poll dates, one firm effect per firm, a Dirichlet poll likelihood with a learned precision law, a pooled isotropic Student-t election-day discrepancy, and a Student-t logit for the ballot share outside the named reference. It reproduces the 2026 forecast of the research integrated model, whose observation-layer machinery it drops, in seconds; the design review, replication and held-out checks are recorded in `docs/research/compact-mayoral-model-2026-09-21.md`.

**Specification frozen for publication.** Isotropic discrepancy, Gaussian innovations, certified-field 2026 polls only, population priors, joint refit. Alternatives were tested and rejected: the leaders-only discrepancy variant is fragile under held-out testing and its fits diverge when the leader scale collapses; Student-t innovations change nothing in the forecast or the held-out record and add divergences; admitting pre-certification polls as conditional compositions shifts the answer by about a point while mis-specifying field changes as movement; naming the certified minor candidates that one poll reports double-counts the residual pool. The named 2026 candidates are the three the certified-field polling established; every other certified candidate is the residual pool, which is listed by count and, where a poll reported them, by name.

**Calibration basis.** The election-day discrepancy scale is learned from the seven historical outcomes. In pre-registered held-out checks (each result nulled in turn and predicted from the polls available 39 and 14 days out), the realized leader-margin error was 16–17 points RMSE at both horizons, with 80% coverage 4/4 and 6/7. This is the reason the published probability of the poll leader winning falls from about 84% under the lightweight model to about 63%: the lightweight model's margin uncertainty (about 9 points) was calibrated on three elections using the eventual runner-up; the compact model's is the pooled historical miss measured against the runner-up known at the time. No poll drove the change and nothing is tuned to a preferred probability (ADR 0030).

**Feed contract, schema 4, policy `margin-first-joint-draws-v1`.** Every public number comes from one set of joint election-day draws: full-ballot vote-share medians with central 80% intervals for the named candidates and the residual pool; the signed leader-minus-challenger margin as fixed five-point bins from −100 to +100 with the probability the challenger finishes ahead; full-race win probabilities, with the residual pool at zero by construction; the polls used; the analysis cutoff; and a model record with the specification, draw count, seed and qualification status. Bands, frequency statements, per-card scenarios, the unsigned margin density and the close-result and incumbent-defeat quantities are dropped. `candidate_win` and `forecast_favourite` keep their shapes (ADR 0051). Two prespecified sensitivity refits (leaders discrepancy; pre-certification polls) travel as audit metadata and are not rendered.

**Qualification gate.** The production fit is four chains of 1,000 warmup and 4,000 retained draws (Monte Carlo error on a win probability under half a point). It is publishable only with zero divergent transitions (one automatic retry at higher target acceptance), split R-hat below 1.01 and bulk ESS of at least 400 on every non-constant coordinate. Otherwise the build fails and the previous release stays live. Sampler diagnostics stay out of the public presentation.

**Presentation.** The frontend renders the margin distribution first, then candidate vote-share ranges, then full-race win probabilities, the hierarchy approved on 2026-09-14 (`toronto-election-poll-tracker/docs/design/forecast-presentation-prototype.md`). Display choices: central 80% intervals, medians, whole-percent probabilities with "<1%" and ">99%" at the tails, compared candidates named by the feed.
