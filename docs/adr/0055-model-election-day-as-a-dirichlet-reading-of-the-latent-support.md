# Model election day as a Dirichlet reading of the latent support

This amends one clause of ADR 0054. The published compact joint model keeps everything ADR 0054 froze (certified-field 2026 polls only, seven historical campaigns, Gaussian random walk on log-odds contrasts, one firm effect per firm, the Dirichlet poll likelihood with its learned precision law, the Student-t residual pool, population priors, joint refit, the fail-closed qualification gate, feed schema 4 and policy `margin-first-joint-draws-v1`) except the election-day discrepancy. That clause was a pooled isotropic Student-t(5) shock on the log-odds contrasts with one scale, `tau_election`, learned across every reference candidate in every campaign. It is replaced by a Dirichlet composition reading: the named election result is drawn as `Dirichlet(phi_election × election_mixing × p)` around the latent named support `p` at election day, with `phi_election ~ LogNormal(log 40, 1.5)` learned jointly and the per-campaign `election_mixing ~ Gamma(5/2, 5/2)` heavy-tail device retained, so campaign-wide shocks of the 2003 kind remain possible. Observed campaigns condition the Dirichlet on their actual named shares; the 2026 campaign draws its result. `tau_election` no longer exists in the published fit.

**Why.** On 2026-09-22 the user asked why this model gave Chow about 63% where the retired lightweight model gave 84%. Both read the current polls identically (Chow +11 over Bradford); the whole gap was the election-day scale, and that scale was inflated by construction. A shock symmetric in log-odds pools the proportional swings of fringe candidates (12 of the corpus's 38 reference candidates finished under 2%, eight of them in 2023) with the front-runners', then applies the pooled scale to Chow and Bradford. Two pre-registered structural alternatives were tested first under a decision rule written before any fit: a leaders-versus-rest scale (fragile, hundreds of divergences whenever 2023 is held out) and a support-scaled log-odds shock. The second found the data's own variance scaling to be p(1−p) (exponent about 0.5 in every fold) but still lost on the leader margin, because a symmetric log-share shock with that scaling drags the leaders' median down after renormalisation (reproduced by simulation). The Dirichlet is the standard composition-noise family with exactly that variance scaling and no location drag, and it is the family the model already uses for every poll. The full record, including the rule, the priors and the expectations written before each round, is `docs/research/compact-mayoral-discrepancy-scaling-2026-09-22.md`.

**Evidence.** Leave-one-campaign-out, identical sampler settings, control = the ADR 0054 model. At the 39-day horizon (four campaigns, the 2026 horizon) the leader-margin CRPS fell from 9.84 to 9.35 points, better in every fold; at 14 days (seven campaigns) from 10.01 to 9.16. Coverage of the 80% margin interval is unchanged (4/4 and 6/7); the winner log score improved at both horizons (0.41 → 0.33, 0.34 → 0.25); per-candidate PIT calibration improved (pooled Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance 0.24 → 0.145); the worst fold had two divergent transitions in 4,000. The rule required all four of clean sampling, a lower 39-day CRPS, a 14-day CRPS within 2% of the control, and calibration no worse; all four held. The 2026 forecast was computed once, after the rule was applied: Chow 70.2%, Bradford 29.3%, Alexander 0.5%; Chow–Bradford margin +11.5 with an 80% interval of −17 to +38 (spread 22.6 points against the previous 28.5).

**What this does not do.** It does not restore the lightweight model's 84%, which rested on three well-polled races and never saw a runner-up collapse; Toronto's record has two, and they stay in the corpus. Seven elections cannot pin the precision closely (posterior 90% interval for `phi_election` is 20–97). The two three-poll campaigns (2003, 2006) score marginally worse under the new model. The sparse-early-polls weakness in the 2018 fold (the model's read of the race at 39 days was Tory +20 against a raw poll average of +34) is a current-state issue shared by every variant and absent for 2026, which has six recent full-field polls. Nothing here was tuned to a preferred probability (ADR 0030): the priors and the rule preceded the fits, and a fourth structural attempt was ruled out in advance.

**Consequences.** The feed's `model.specification.discrepancy` reads `dirichlet`. The audit sensitivity refit `leaders-discrepancy` is replaced by `isotropic-discrepancy`, the ADR 0054 specification, so each release records what the previous model would have said; `with-pre-certification-polls` is unchanged. The frontend needs no change: schema 4 is untouched. The methodology page's description of election-day error is updated. First release under this ADR: `backend-2026-09-22.1`. Research code and the 1.7 GB of held-out run artifacts stay untracked.
