Where does "other" support go?

Prototype for the Suspended Campaign map (C and D). Throwaway, not production.

The question. When a candidate ends their campaign but stays on the ballot, where should their supporters' votes go in the forecast, and what should we do with the "someone else" answers in polls? This page compares four rules on the two real cases we have polls for: Chris Alexander (2026) and Sarah Thomson (2010).
  • A. Split in proportion (what both current versions do): the departed candidate keeps a small share; the rest goes to the other candidates in proportion to their own support.
  • B. Carry forward (the old model): the departed candidate keeps their whole last poll share to election day.
  • C. Learn the split: compare the same pollster's polls before and after the exit, and learn from that where the support went.
  • D. Count "someone else" as real support: other candidates' election-day share comes from the polls' "someone else" answers instead of past elections.
This is illustrative arithmetic, not the forecast model. It starts from the model's real forecast before each exit and applies each rule's logic to one pair of polls from the same pollster. The model's real numbers differ; compare the rules with each other.
