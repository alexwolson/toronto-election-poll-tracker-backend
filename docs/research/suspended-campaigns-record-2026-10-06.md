# Suspended campaigns in the record: retained share, transfers, pollster practice

October 6, 2026. Research note for issue #29. It gives context for model and editorial
decisions and is not a model input. Nothing here changes the forecast.

**Question.** On Tuesday, October 6, 2026, Chris Alexander publicly ended his Toronto
mayoral campaign. The Ontario withdrawal deadline had passed (2 p.m., August 21, 2026), so
his name stays on the October 26 ballot. Before he quit he polled about 4–10% (figures
from the issue). The project calls this a **Suspended Campaign**. This note asks three
things about earlier suspended campaigns:

- How much of a candidate's polled share survived to the count?
- Where did the rest go?
- How did pollsters handle the candidate afterwards?

The US is out of scope. Canadian cases turned out to be plentiful enough, so no other
countries were added.

**Labels.** OBSERVED means read in a primary document or computed from one. INFERRED means
my own reasoning or arithmetic on those figures. Share bases differ between pollsters:
"all" means all respondents including undecided, and "decided" means decided voters (with
or without leaners). "Normalized" means the share divided by (100 − undecided), which is
INFERRED arithmetic.

**Main findings**

- **Retained share.** In every case the suspended candidate finished between 0.05% and
  1.3% of the vote. As a fraction of the last pre-suspension poll share, that is about 3%
  of it (Thomson) up to about 25% (Fortier). This is INFERRED arithmetic, set out below.
- **Size comparison.** The only cases near Alexander's size are Thomson and Rossi in
  mid-September 2010, when they polled 6–11%.
- **Pollster practice.** Every firm that published after a suspension in these cases
  stopped reporting the candidate separately. The one exception is Forum's June 23, 2023
  Toronto poll, which kept Davis on its list at 0%. No Canadian release was found that
  asked the question both ways after a suspension.

## Rules that put these names on the ballot

- **Ontario, 2026.** "If you decide to withdraw your nomination, you must notify the clerk
  in writing by the close of nominations (2 p.m. August 21, 2026)"
  ([Ontario 2026 candidates' guide, p. 6](https://www.ontario.ca/files/2026-03/mmah-2026-candidates-guide-en-2026-03-31.pdf)).
  OBSERVED.
- **Ontario, earlier cycles.** Nominations closed on September 10, 2010
  ([Wikipedia, 2010 Toronto mayoral election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Toronto_mayoral_election))
  and September 12, 2014
  ([Wikipedia, 2014 London election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_London,_Ontario,_municipal_election)).
- **Calgary 2010.** No statute was read. CBC reported that "the ballots for the municipal
  vote have already been printed so Hawkesworth's name will still appear on them, election
  officials said"
  ([CBC, Oct 13, 2010](https://web.archive.org/web/20150919124348/http://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/hawkesworth-drops-out-backs-higgins-1.971147)).
  OBSERVED.
- **Montreal 2017.** Fortier "abandoned his campaign"; his name officially stayed on the
  ballot
  ([Wikipedia, 2017 Montreal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Montreal_municipal_election)).
  The official count shows him receiving votes.

## 1. Toronto 2010: Thomson and Rossi

### Facts

**Sarah Thomson**

- She ended her campaign on September 28, 2010, and asked supporters to vote for George
  Smitherman: "George Smitherman offers the voters a viable and responsible alternative"
  ([blogTO, Sep 28, 2010](https://www.blogto.com/city/2010/09/sarah_thomson_drops_out_of_mayoral_race/)).
- The same piece said it was "basically impossible" that all seven per cent of her polled
  support would reach Smitherman, because her name stayed on the ballot.
- Ipsos Reid's next release said: "Candidate Sara Thomson dropped out of the race and threw
  her support behind George Smitherman (but her name will still appear on the ballot)"
  ([Ipsos, Oct 13, 2010](https://www.ipsos.com/en-ca/heading-down-back-stretch-its-horserace-toronto-mayor-edge-smitherman)).
  OBSERVED.

**Rocco Rossi**

- He ended his campaign shortly after 9 p.m. on October 13, 2010. That afternoon a poll had
  put him at 4%. He did not endorse anyone
  ([blogTO, Oct 13, 2010](https://www.blogto.com/news_flash/2010/10/rocco_rossi_pulls_out_of_the_mayoral_race/)).
- The Globe reported that he blamed polarization: "The oxygen in the middle was just being
  sucked out." It also reported that his former campaign manager Sachin Aggarwal and other
  volunteers moved to Smitherman's camp
  ([Globe and Mail, Oct 14, 2010](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/news/toronto/oxygen-in-the-middle-sucked-out-rossi-says/article1214950/)).
  OBSERVED.

**Official result**

The City Clerk's declaration, dated October 28, 2010, gives Ford 383,501, Smitherman
289,832, Pantalone 95,482, Rossi 5,012 and Thomson 1,883
([City of Toronto declaration](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/9783-election-2010-clerksofficialdeclaration.pdf)).
Summing the City's poll-by-poll mayoral workbook gives a total of 813,984 valid votes
([City open data, 2010 results](https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/election-results-official/)).
That makes the shares Ford 47.11%, Smitherman 35.61%, Pantalone 11.73%, Rossi 0.616% and
Thomson 0.231%. OBSERVED. These match `mayoral_outcomes.csv` exactly.

### Where the support went

**What the pollsters themselves said** (OBSERVED):

- Angus Reid, Oct 18: "Smitherman appears to be the main beneficiary from the early exits of
  candidates Sarah Thomson and Rocco Rossi." Its headline: "Support for Smitherman increases
  by 14 points following recent withdrawals."
- Nanos, Oct 14–16 recap: "with the dropping out of the race of Sara Thomson and Rocco Rossi,
  the anti-Ford forces have coalesced around George Smitherman", and "the change in the
  structure of the race has worked to the primary benefit of Smitherman." Nanos found
  Pantalone's support unchanged "as candidates have dropped out."

**Same-firm changes across each suspension** (INFERRED arithmetic; sampling error is about
±5 points per Ipsos wave and larger for differences):

- **Ipsos, Sep 24–26 to Oct 8–10, all adults, n=400 each** (covers Thomson's suspension):
  - Smitherman +8 and Ford +2.
  - Pantalone +1 and Rossi −3.
  - Undecided +8.
  - Thomson's 7 points cannot be traced. They are consistent with a split between
    Smitherman and undecided, but this design cannot separate that from ordinary movement.
- **Angus Reid, Sep 14–15 to Oct 14–15, decided + leaners** (covers both suspensions):
  - The combined 19 points for Thomson and Rossi went to zero.
  - Smitherman +14, Pantalone +3, Ford +2.
- **Nanos, Sep 14–16 to Oct 14–16, decided** (covers both suspensions):
  - The combined 16.1 points went to zero.
  - Smitherman +19.2, Ford −1.9, Pantalone −1.8.

**Retained share** (INFERRED):

- Thomson kept 0.231 ÷ 8.4, about **3%** of her last normalized poll share. She suspended 27
  days out and endorsed a rival.
- Rossi kept 0.616 ÷ 5.3, about **12%**, or about 18% against the likely-voter base. He
  suspended 12 days out and endorsed nobody.

**Advance votes** (OBSERVED from the City workbook). Treating subdivisions 93–99 as advance
polls gives 76,246 valid mayoral votes. That is within 1.5% of the 77,349–77,365 advance
ballots cited in `advance-voting-and-forecast-target.md`.

- Rossi took **1.60%** of advance votes but **0.51%** on election day.
- Thomson took 0.31% of advance votes and 0.22% on election day.
- The 2010 advance vote ran October 5–8, October 12–13 and October 16–17
  ([by-law 1258-2009](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/bylaws/2009/law1258.pdf)). Most of it
  came before Rossi quit on the evening of October 13 and all of it came after Thomson quit.
- INFERRED: part of Rossi's 0.62% was cast while he was still campaigning, so his true
  post-suspension retention is lower than 12%.

**How small the retained vote is** (OBSERVED). Several fringe candidates with no polling
presence out-polled Thomson: George Babula 0.40%, Rocco Achampong 0.34%, Abdullah-Baquie
Ghazi 0.34%, Michael Alexander 0.30%. INFERRED: Thomson's count is at the floor that any
name on a 40-name ballot collects.

## 3. Pollster practice after a suspension

| Case | Polls published after the suspension | Was the candidate still offered? |
|---|---|---|
| Toronto 2010, Thomson | Ipsos Reid, Oct 8–10 | **Dropped.** The tables list Rossi, Smitherman, Pantalone, Ford, "Other candidate" and Undecided, with no Thomson row ([tables](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/publication/2010-10/4995-1tb1.pdf)). The release text still told readers her name would appear on the ballot. OBSERVED. |

## Self-critique: how these numbers could mislead

- **Mixed bases.** The pre-suspension shares are on different bases (all, decided, likely)
  and were often taken weeks before the exit. Fortier's last poll was four weeks out. The
  retained-share ratios (3–25%) carry that noise and should be read as orders of magnitude.
- **Ballot-presence floor.** On long ballots, minor names collect 0.2–0.6% with no campaign
  at all (Toronto 2010 fringe, London's Kaplansky at 0.63%). Much of what looks like
  "retained" vote may be this floor rather than loyalty. If so, the ratio depends on the
  candidate's poll share only weakly.
- **Votes cast before the exit.** Advance and mail votes cast before a suspension inflate
  the count. Rossi took three times his election-day share in the advance vote. Cases with
  large early voting shares will overstate post-suspension retention.
- **Selection.** Cases were found through Wikipedia mentions, so low-profile suspensions
  could be missing. Only Thomson and Rossi polled near Alexander's level. The others polled
  1–8%.
- **Transfer evidence is weak.** All of it comes from same-firm comparisons across waves.
  Those comparisons cannot separate exit effects from general campaign movement. In Toronto
  2010, for example, undecided rose by 8 points in the same Ipsos interval.
