# Mainstreet September head-to-head: consolidation leaves Chow ahead

October 2, 2026. Descriptive primary-source review for the editorial scenarios.
No ingestion, production feed, model likelihood or forecast changed.

## Finding

Mainstreet's September 28–29 survey leaves Chow ahead by approximately six points
whether respondents are offered the full published field or only Chow and
Bradford. In the alternate field, both gain almost equally on the same
all-respondent denominator. Among respondents naming one of those two candidates,
Bradford gains about 0.67 percentage points of share. This latest evidence does
not reproduce the Bradford lead in Mainstreet's June hypothetical two-way result.
It also does not measure what Alexander's supporters would do if he suspended his
campaign. [First-party September report, physical pp. 3–4 and 13–14](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/66c8dfb086a015b3b519e988/6abfcf262aaff1cac7ed6457_Mainstreet_City_of_Toronto_September_28-29_2026.pdf).

## Comparable denominators

The September survey recruited 1,000 Toronto adults using landline and cellular
IVR. Both relevant tables report unweighted and weighted bases of 1,000. The
report describes demographic weighting and a full-sample margin of error of
±3.1 points at 95%; this does not supply the uncertainty of the *paired change*
between questions. [September report, physical pp. 2, 4 and 14](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/66c8dfb086a015b3b519e988/6abfcf262aaff1cac7ed6457_Mainstreet_City_of_Toronto_September_28-29_2026.pdf).

| All respondents | Full published field | Chow–Bradford only | Difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Chow | 38.1% | 47.1% | +9.0pp |
| Bradford | 32.2% | 40.9% | +8.7pp |
| Undecided | 16.0% | 12.0% | −4.0pp |
| Chow's lead | 5.9pp | 6.2pp | +0.3pp |

The full field's other choices total 13.7%: Alexander 7.2%, Parker 1.8%, McVie
2.0%, and another candidate 2.7%. The alternate question therefore changes the
options for **all** these supporters, alongside a reduction in undecideds; it
does not remove Alexander alone. The 17.7-point combined net gain for the top two
equals the 13.7-point removed candidate field plus the 4.0-point net reduction in
undecided responses. This is aggregate accounting, not identified individual
transfers. [September report, physical pp. 3 and 13](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/66c8dfb086a015b3b519e988/6abfcf262aaff1cac7ed6457_Mainstreet_City_of_Toronto_September_28-29_2026.pdf).

For a second comparison, normalize each table over Chow plus Bradford only:

| Share within Chow plus Bradford | Full published field | Chow–Bradford only | Difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Chow | 54.196% | 53.523% | −0.674pp |
| Bradford | 45.804% | 46.477% | +0.674pp |
| Chow's lead | 8.393pp | 7.045pp | −1.347pp |

These are calculations from the printed shares, not new poll estimates. For
example, full-field Chow is `38.1 / (38.1 + 32.2)` and alternate-field Chow is
`47.1 / (47.1 + 40.9)`. The different direction of the raw-gap and normalized-gap
change arises because the number naming Chow or Bradford rises from 70.3% to
88.0%. Comparing the full-field decided/leaning headline (Chow 45.4%, Bradford
38.3%) directly with the alternate-field all-respondent numbers would instead
mix denominators. [September report, physical pp. 3, 8 and 13](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/66c8dfb086a015b3b519e988/6abfcf262aaff1cac7ed6457_Mainstreet_City_of_Toronto_September_28-29_2026.pdf).

For the website's candidate-choice view, remove undecided but keep all candidate
choices (including Other) in the denominator. The full field becomes Chow
45.357%, Bradford 38.333%, a 7.024-point lead; the head-to-head becomes Chow
53.523%, Bradford 46.477%, a 7.045-point lead. Thus Chow's lead is also almost
unchanged on the expressed-choice basis. The separately printed full-field
decided/leaning numbers differ slightly from this conversion due to rounding.
These transformations are descriptive comparisons, not additional observations.

## Previous evidence

**Forum July 29:** the detailed tables show Chow 48%, Bradford 36%, and someone
else 17% without Alexander offered; with Alexander offered they show Chow 47%,
Bradford 32%, Alexander 11%, and someone else 10%. The source prose differs from
the table on Bradford's first figure and the second residual; use the detailed
tables consistently. The questions share recruitment but have decided/leaning
unweighted bases of 887 and 889 (weighted 888 and 890), so their denominator
*definition* matches but their retained respondents need not. They are not the
same all-respondent comparison as September. The four-point Bradford difference
and one-point Chow difference motivated the illustrative one-third/one-tenth
Alexander allocation, but do not identify those fractions as observed individual
transfer rates. [Forum July report, physical pp. 3–4](https://www.forumresearch.com/news/attachments/fb26f543-8af1-44fa-b85b-3e7592b84b8d.pdf).

**Mainstreet June 12–18:** the published hypothetical two-way outcome was Chow
48.1%, Bradford 51.9%, with no undecided category, compared with the post-leaning
all-respondent field at Chow 36.2%, Bradford 31.4%, someone else 15.5%, and
undecided 17.0%. The release identifies someone-else respondents as follow-up
recipients without establishing exclusive routing or the treatment of remaining
undecideds. Alexander was not a named option. Thus neither June's Bradford
majority nor September's Chow lead can be interpreted as an Alexander-voter
preference split, and their different construction and unresolved categories
limit a claim of change over time. [Mainstreet June release](https://www.mainstreetresearch.ca/post/bradford-and-chow-matchup-could-be-close);
[retained June source review](mayoral-mainstreet-june-two-way-primary-review-2026-09-14.md);
[routing and arithmetic investigation](mayoral-mainstreet-june-two-way-results-2026-09-14.md).

## Implication for the withdrawal scenario

The latest poll supports saying that a two-candidate choice still leaves Chow
ahead. It gives little support to a claim that removing the rest of the field
would automatically put Bradford level with her. It does **not** replace the
one-third/one-tenth scenario with a measured Alexander allocation: ordinary
supporters may switch, undecideds may choose, and each other candidate's
supporters may behave differently. There are no published cross-tabs linking a
person's first candidate choice to their head-to-head answer in this table book.
Demographic cross-tabs do not establish that link. The report does not publish a
complete routing/rotation script. [September report, physical pp. 3–17](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/66c8dfb086a015b3b519e988/6abfcf262aaff1cac7ed6457_Mainstreet_City_of_Toronto_September_28-29_2026.pdf).

This is one recruitment with dependent readings, not two independent polls. Do
not add the alternate field to the normal average as if it were another sample,
or substitute its shares for full-field shares. Incorporating it into a forecast
would require an explicit relationship between actual-field support and
hypothetical-field answers, preserving their shared respondents. This note
performs no such model change.
