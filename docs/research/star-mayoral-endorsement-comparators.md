# Toronto Star mayoral endorsements as comparators for Bailão in 2023

**Research date:** September 9, 2026.
**Scope:** historical mayoral endorsements, their timing, and recoverable polling around them. This is an evidence audit, not an endorsement-effect estimate or production-data change.

## Finding

Historical Star endorsements can provide context for the 2023 surge, but the recovered mayoral record does **not supply an estimated newspaper-only effect that we can subtract from Bailão's increase**. The best available comparison is John Tory in 2014: around the Star endorsement, Forum measured **43% → 44%** and Mainstreet **43% → 42% among decided voters**, or **38% → 38% among all respondents**. Those are small observed changes from one campaign, subject to other campaign events and polling error. They do not prove that the Star's contribution was zero in 2014 or 2023. [Forum final report](https://web.archive.org/web/20141027191412id_/http://poll.forumresearch.com/data/TO%20Horserace%20News%20Release%20%282014%2010%2026%29%20Forum%20Research.pdf), [Mainstreet October 17 report](https://www.scribd.com/document/243313481), [Mainstreet October 24 report](https://www.scribd.com/document/244228439).

The other recent cycles lack a clean same-firm before/after pair in the recovered corpus. Several stored endorsement dates also require correction or qualification before an event-study design could use them.

## The useful 2014 comparison

These figures were checked against the retained first-party PDFs, not the normalized Wikipedia proxy. Dates below are fieldwork dates.

| Firm / population | Before | After | Observed Tory change |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Forum, likely decided/leaning voters and people who already voted | October 20, n=852: **43%** | October 25, n=986: **44%** | **+1 percentage point** |
| Mainstreet, decided respondents | October 16, parent n=2,265: **43%** | October 23, parent n=3,569: **42%** | **−1 point** |
| Mainstreet, all respondents, including undecided | Same October 16 sample: **38%** | Same October 23 sample: **38%** | **0 points** |

Sources: [Forum October 20 wave](https://web.archive.org/web/20141022142832id_/http://poll.forumresearch.com/data/TO%20Horserace%20News%20Release%20%282014%2010%2020%29%20Forum%20Research.pdf), [Forum October 25 wave](https://web.archive.org/web/20141027191412id_/http://poll.forumresearch.com/data/TO%20Horserace%20News%20Release%20%282014%2010%2026%29%20Forum%20Research.pdf), [Mainstreet October 16 wave](https://www.scribd.com/document/243313481), [Mainstreet October 23 wave](https://www.scribd.com/document/244228439). Mainstreet's two rows are dependent readings of the same surveys, not two independent replications. The parent sample sizes are not established decided-voter bases.

Forum's final report itself describes its October 25 shares as similar to October 20. Its previous October 15 Tory reading was **39%**, so Tory had already gained four points before the Star window. Simply treating the entire late rise as newspaper persuasion would make the same pretrend mistake that we want to avoid with Bailão. [Forum October 20 and October 25 reports, above].

The comparison has material limits:

- **Other endorsements cluster in the interval.** The Globe and Sun backed Tory shortly before the Star. The Globe's public editor confirms its own endorsement in [her first-party account](https://j-source.ca/globe-public-editor-why-newspapers-endorse-political-candidates/); the National Post's October 24 endorsement is another primary-source lead inside the Forum interval, listed in the [2014 election reference inventory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Toronto_mayoral_election#Endorsements). The Mainstreet October 16–23 interval also spans the Globe/Sun events. This is not a Star-only treatment window.
- **Methods are not perfectly fixed.** Mainstreet's October 24 report explicitly states geographic weighting; the October 17 report does not document it. Both include an other-candidate option and already-voted language introduced by the October 17 release. An earlier Mainstreet wave would introduce additional questionnaire changes. [Mainstreet reports, above].
- **The candidates occupy different positions.** Tory was already leading in 2014; Bailão was a trailing challenger in 2023. A newspaper endorsement could operate differently when it validates a leader versus helps coordinate a fragmented challenge. This is a transportability concern, not an estimated adjustment.
- **No individual exposure or counterfactual is observed.** A flat aggregate series can hide a positive endorsement response offset by other influences. A one-point change can be sampling or other survey error. Therefore these rows neither estimate a zero Star effect nor bound its 2023 contribution.

## Why the other cycles do not currently add replications

The [canonical historical samples](../../data/raw/polls/historical_mayoral/poll_samples.csv), [readings](../../data/raw/polls/historical_mayoral/poll_readings.csv), [responses](../../data/raw/polls/historical_mayoral/poll_responses.csv), and [source manifest](../../data/raw/polls/historical_mayoral/source_documents.csv) establish the following availability limits:

- **2010:** the recovered EKOS surveys cover **October 13–21** and **October 20–22**, with Smitherman at **35.6%** and **33.3%** among decided/leaning voters. The earlier interval already includes the endorsement and the intervals overlap. Their difference is not untreated-to-treated movement. Angus Reid's October 14–15 poll is pre-event, but there is no post-event Angus Reid counterpart in the corpus. [EKOS October 22 report](https://www.ekospolitics.com/wp-content/uploads/full_report_october22.pdf), [EKOS October 24 addendum](https://www.ekospolitics.com/wp-content/uploads/full_report_october_24.pdf).
- **2018:** the latest recovered fieldwork is DART **October 12–15**, before either candidate Star date. The last recovered Forum wave ends October 10, and Mainstreet ends September 25. The legacy discovery table also has no later wave. DART's final head-to-head reading is not an after-endorsement observation. [DART factum](https://web.archive.org/web/20181023234340id_/http://dartincom.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Sun-Toronto-Election-Factum-1-Oct-2018.pdf).
- **2022:** the only recovered citywide sample is Forum **October 7–8**, well before October 22. A poll-to-election difference would combine endorsement response, turnout, polling error and all other late events. [Forum report](https://poll.forumresearch.com/data/d8c2ba39-b222-4066-a9c4-5b2421fb82abGeneral%20Mayoral%20News%20Release%20Final.pdf).
- **2003/2006:** the last recovered fieldwork ends **November 2, 2003**, and **November 5, 2006**, respectively. Neither supplies an after-endorsement survey under the date leads above.

These are limitations of the inspected/recovered record, not claims that no unpublished poll exists.

## Implication for isolating Tory's contribution

A defensible next step is to estimate Bailão's **excess movement over explicitly chosen pre-endorsement trends**, then show how the inferred Tory component changes under different assumptions about the Star, independently occurring consolidation, and interaction between the endorsements. The result is an assumption-dependent decomposition.

The 2014 comparator makes it unreasonable to assume that every Star endorsement mechanically produces a Bailão-sized surge. It does **not** supply a defensible fixed Star coefficient, and subtracting its observed −1 to +1 point changes from the 2023 residual would create false precision. The interaction also matters: a joint signal from Tory and the Star can have an effect different from the sum of either signal alone. Even an excellent historical estimate of the average newspaper response would not by itself identify that interaction in June 2023.

To improve this part of the design, the missing information is specific: verified first-public timestamps; any post-endorsement 2018/2022 polling; disaggregated daily 2010 interviews; and comparable voter panels or measured/randomized endorsement exposure. Council endorsements require a separate audit of timing and before/after outcomes; their number alone cannot solve the mayoral identification problem.
