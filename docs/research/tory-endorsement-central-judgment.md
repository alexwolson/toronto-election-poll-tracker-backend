# Central judgment for a hypothetical Tory endorsement

As of September 9, 2026. This is an analyst judgment informed by the research, **not a fitted causal effect, validated forecast, or probability distribution**.

## Prediction and horizon

Assume a prominent public Tory endorsement soon, both Bradford and Alexander remain in the race, and no simultaneous withdrawal, matching Star endorsement or other major campaign shock. The numerical judgment describes the first one to two weeks after the announcement relative to an otherwise unchanged baseline. It does not assume the same movement persists to October 26.

My central working expectation is **about +3 percentage points for Bradford if endorsed**, or **about +5 for Alexander if endorsed**. I expect a Bradford endorsement to strengthen the existing leading challenger and narrow Chow's lead. An Alexander endorsement could give its recipient a larger personal gain while leaving the challengers more evenly divided, increasing Chow's margin over her nearest opponent. Chow remains ahead in both central illustrations.

I have greater confidence in that comparative mechanism than in the difference between three and five points. Neither the relative size of the two effects nor their source allocation is identified by our historical evidence. Zero effect or a negative response remains possible. For sensitivity work, use 0–6 points for Bradford and 0–10 for Alexander, alongside an adverse-response case. These are chosen stress ranges, not confidence or credible intervals, and not exhaustive bounds.

## Explicit central source assumptions

| Hypothetical event | Chow change | Bradford change | Alexander change | Other change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Tory endorses Bradford | −1.5 pp | +3.0 pp | −1.5 pp | 0 |
| Tory endorses Alexander | −0.5 pp | −4.0 pp | +5.0 pp | −0.5 pp |

The first row assumes roughly 15% of Alexander's baseline support transfers, with equal additional support from Chow. The second assumes roughly 10% of Bradford's support transfers, with a smaller gain from Chow and Other. These allocations are **judgment calls**, not inferred individual switching rates. In particular, the evidence does not establish that a Bradford endorsement would draw half its gain from Chow.

## Why these are reasonable working assumptions

- **Historical scale:** our [pretrend calculations](endorsement-identification/README.md) leave approximately four citywide points above Bailão's recent trend in the first subsequent polls. That includes Tory, the Star, other events, interaction and error. It supports exploring effects of a few points, but supplies no Tory-specific estimate.
- **Existing consolidation:** Bradford starts with 39/49, or 79.6%, of the two challengers' combined support in the new release. He has less rival support available to absorb. Alexander has a much larger rival pool, but attracting enough of it to replace Bradford is a substantial coordination change. A pure transfer requires 37.2% of Bradford's baseline support just to tie him within the pair.
- **Transfer scale:** the historical [concentration statistics](tory-consolidation-dynamics.md) suggest examining roughly 10–17% of rival support as one transparent analogy, while the fragile pretrend-adjusted figures are smaller. The new central source assumptions use the low-to-middle part of those raw descriptive scales. They do not carry over the whole 2023 poll-to-election swing.
- **Outside-pool limits:** the [Alexander entry evidence](alexander-entry-dynamics.md) shows a large net reduction in “someone else,” with little of that category remaining today. The central Alexander illustration therefore uses a different, rival-heavy mix; it does not recycle the original entry gain. This change of mix is assumed, not estimated.
- **Recognition:** the new Liaison release reports 46% still unfamiliar with Alexander versus 24% for Bradford. That makes a larger recognition benefit for Alexander plausible, but does not measure endorsement responsiveness or prove a larger gain.
- **Star comparisons:** our mayoral and council comparator work supplies no defensible newspaper coefficient to subtract. We should not present its failure to isolate a large Star effect as evidence that Tory caused the entire residual.

The main uncertainty is whether an endorsement creates sustained coordination that changes which challenger voters consider viable. The central view does not assume an Alexander surge large enough to reverse the leading-challenger ordering, nor complete consolidation behind Bradford. Greater consolidation and outside-pool recruitment remain upside scenarios. A rival withdrawal would change the question and require a separate assessment.
