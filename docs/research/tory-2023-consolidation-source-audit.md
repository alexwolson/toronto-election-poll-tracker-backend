# Selected-candidate consolidation around Tory's 2023 endorsement

**Research date:** 2026-09-09
**Scope:** Primary Liaison and Forum polls around June 21, 2023, and certified final votes. Research only; no implementation or canonical input changes.

## Finding

The result is consistent with substantial late consolidation around Bailão, but the public data do not measure a fraction of rival voters who transferred to her because of Tory. The selected rival pool fell much more between pre-endorsement polling and the election result than between comparable pre/post polls. A fractional-transfer scenario therefore needs an explicit endpoint and an explicit assumption; the historical record does not supply a unique calibrated transfer rate.

Define the **selected rival pool** as Saunders + Furey + Bradford, and the **combined selected pool** as those three plus Bailão. These are candidate groupings chosen for this analysis. They do not measure voter ideology, establish that all supporters were on the right or centre, cover every possible non-Chow voter, or imply a closed group of people whose choices only moved among its members.

The two adjacent comparisons tell different stories:

- **Before → first after:** Bailão **+5 points**, selected rivals **+1**, combined pool **+6**. The first measured bump cannot be described as an observed five-point net transfer out of these three rivals.
- **First after → final poll:** Bailão **+5**, selected rivals **−4**, combined pool **+1**.
- **Before → final poll:** Bailão **+10**, selected rivals **−3**, combined pool **+7**. The rivals' net share declined by **10% of their initial 30-point pool**, while Bailão's gain was **33.33% of that initial pool**. Those are different descriptive ratios.

Bailão received **68.6652% of the combined selected pool's final votes**. That is a final allocation among selected candidates, not a measure of who switched or why.

| Comparison to certified result | Bailão gain | Net rival-pool decline | Rival decline / initial rival pool | Bailão gain / initial rival pool |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Liaison June 17–18 baseline | +20.4541 pp | 15.1898 pp | **50.63%** | **68.18%** |
| Forum June 16 baseline | +19.4541 pp | 17.1898 pp | **53.72%** | **60.79%** |

These large poll-to-result ratios combine survey error, the eventual turnout composition, undecided resolution, changing choices, and all subsequent campaign information. They are not estimates of a Tory conversion probability. The combined pool itself changes: 42→47.2643 in the Liaison comparison and 45→47.2643 in Forum. A model that transfers a fixed fraction solely within this pool deliberately imposes a simplifying assumption the observed aggregates do not satisfy exactly.

## What can be carried into a scenario

The source record supports the question **“What happens if an endorsement helps one challenger attract a specified fraction of selected rivals' support?”** It supports testing modest through substantial fractions and reporting how sensitive the result is to the chosen pool and timing. It does not justify labelling any one of those fractions “the Tory effect.”

Keep three quantities separate: the recipient's increase; the rivals' net decrease; and actual gross transfers. Repeated cross-sectional marginals observe the first two, not the third. Even perfectly measured rival losses could go to other candidates or non-voting, while Bailão could simultaneously gain from elsewhere.

Finally, **Tory and the Star endorsed Bailão on June 21**. These short before/after windows contain both announcements. If they caused strategic consolidation, that mechanism belongs to their total effect; independently occurring consolidation remains an alternative explanation. The sources do not isolate Tory, separate the Star contribution, or measure their interaction. The [original event audit](john-tory-2023-endorsement-evidence.md#event-and-competing-chronology) retains the campaign-primary endorsement evidence and timing. A scenario can represent consolidation while leaving its cause and magnitude explicitly assumed.
