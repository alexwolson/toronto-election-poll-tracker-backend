# John Tory's 2023 endorsement of Ana Bailão: evidence and modeling limits

**Research date:** September 9, 2026
**Scope:** the June 2023 event, comparable polling, transferability to 2026, and a proposed conditional scenario analysis for Bradford and Alexander. This is a research memo, not a forecast adjustment or data ingestion.

## Finding

The record supports a substantial late increase in Bailão's support around Tory's endorsement. It does **not identify the number of votes caused by Tory**, or an effect that can simply be transferred to Brad Bradford or Chris Alexander.

The strongest recoverable primary-source comparisons are **+5 percentage points in Liaison's first post-event poll** and **+7 points in Forum's weekly series**. Liaison subsequently measured a further five-point increase. Mainstreet's contemporaneously reported first post-event increase was nine points, but its retained source for that wave is a news article rather than the full pollster report. These are changes over different intervals, not independent estimates of one endorsement coefficient. Sources and denominators are set out below.

This history can anchor the scale of an explicitly assumed scenario. It cannot, by itself, justify an expected Tory boost, a probability distribution for that boost, or a claim that either hypothetical recipient would experience Bailão's result.

## Why the increase is not a causal estimate

The following are analytical conclusions about the available design, not additional findings claimed by a pollster.

1. **There is no observed no-endorsement counterfactual.** We observe Bailão after the endorsement, but not what her support would have been at that same time without it. Repeated polls improve measurement of the trajectory; they do not create the missing comparison.
2. **Momentum predates the endorsement.** Forum's Bailão series was 8% on June 2, 10% on June 9, and 13% on June 16. A flat June 16 baseline produces a seven-point June 23 increase. Mechanically continuing the previous week's three-point increase produces a 16% baseline and a four-point excess. Neither baseline is established as the true counterfactual. The sensitivity shows why seven points cannot be presented as Tory's measured contribution. [Forum trend table](https://poll.forumresearch.com/data/cb12c41a-8453-40e6-90f1-7558d098f7e1Chow%20lead%20narrowing%20in%20final%20days%20of%20campaign_June%2024%202023.pdf).
3. **Other information arrived in the same window.** The Star endorsed the same candidate on the same date. The short series cannot separate their contributions or their interaction. Additional campaign activity and rival responses also belong in the counterfactual.
4. **Persuasion and coordination are different possible mechanisms.** Tory may have changed assessments of Bailão, or helped voters coordinate around a viable alternative. If the endorsement caused strategic consolidation, that consolidation is part of its total effect. Independently occurring late consolidation is a competing explanation. The public data cannot distinguish them.
5. **Aggregate changes do not reveal transfers.** In Liaison, Chow rose while Bailão rose. In Forum, Chow fell. A rise for Bailão cannot be allocated to former Chow, Saunders, Bradford, other-candidate, or undecided voters from these cross-sections. An endorsement's effect on the margin depends on those flows as well as the recipient's gain.
6. **The samples and voting electorate differ.** The polling releases are cross-sectional snapshots with sampling, coverage, weighting, and turnout error. The final election result also includes later decisions. A poll-to-result difference combines these terms and cannot be assigned to the endorsement.
7. **Voting early is not random assignment to an untreated group.** Early and election-day voters differ in ways that may affect both candidate choice and response to political information. Ward-level comparisons add demographic and geographic confounding, and cannot identify individual switching.
8. **One selected successful-looking episode cannot estimate a transferable distribution.** Selecting the case because it was followed by a surge overstates what is known about an ordinary endorsement. A causal contribution of zero, or even an adverse contribution masked by other gains, is not excluded by this design. The observed increase is not a formal upper bound either: offsetting negative influences could conceal a larger effect.

The outcome should therefore be reported as approximately **32.5% for Bailão and 37.2% for Chow**, or by the certified vote counts. Taking Bailão's final share minus a pre-event 12–13% poll gives roughly twenty points, but **that is not an endorsement estimate**. The canonical candidate results sum to 724,638 valid votes; the City separately reports 725,333 total ballots. Do not use total ballots as the candidate-share denominator. [Official declaration](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/900e-Declaration-of-Results-for-the-2023-Toronto-By-Election-for-Mayor.pdf), [City certification release](https://www.toronto.ca/news/toronto-city-clerk-certifies-by-election-for-mayor-results-and-declares-olivia-chow-as-mayor-elect/), and [canonical outcome rows](../../data/raw/elections/mayoral_outcomes.csv).

## Direct evidence of endorsement response

No randomized endorsement experiment, before/after voter panel, or direct measured Tory-exposure effect was found in the inspected public sources or recovered ballot-reading inventory. This is a statement about this search, not proof that no private study exists. Forum's earlier hypothetical-Tory ballot questions ask about **Tory himself running**; they do not estimate what happens when he endorses somebody else. The current corpus distinguishes those questions in [its reading inventory](../../data/raw/polls/historical_mayoral/poll_readings.csv).

The full June 25 Mainstreet report contains ordinary vote intention, second choice, views about a Chow mayoralty, and voting-plan questions. It contains no Tory endorsement treatment. Its last page usefully separates people reporting an earlier vote from people planning to vote on election day, but that is an observational comparison with self-selected timing. It should not be converted into a causal before/after estimate. [Mainstreet report](../../../toronto-election-poll-tracker-data/data/source_documents/historical_mayoral/mainstreet_2023/mainstreet_2023-06-25_report.pdf).

The existing [incumbency research, section 8](mayoral-incumbency-prior.md#8-endorsement-event-audit-john-tory-and-ana-bailão-2023) reaches the same causal limitation. This memo updates that audit with the resolved Liaison typo, the supplied Mainstreet June 25 report, explicit source conflicts, and baseline sensitivity. The repository's [ADR 0007](../adr/0007-admit-political-signals-only-by-validation.md) and [ADR 0040](../adr/0040-exclude-by-elections-from-the-qualification-gate.md) govern any future predictive integration; this evidence review does not constitute that validation.


## Modeling judgment for Bradford and Alexander in 2026

**Recommendation: build an explicitly conditional sensitivity analysis if this question is pursued; do not fit or insert a Tory endorsement coefficient from this one episode.** The 2023 record gives the exercise a relevant historical scale and illustrates possible late consolidation. It does not identify a transferable treatment effect, its sign in 2026, its probability distribution, or a difference in responsiveness between Bradford and Alexander. The recommendations below are proposed analysis choices, not fitted findings.

### Why the candidates cannot simply inherit Bailão's increase

The recipients start in different positions. [Pallas's August 19–21, 2026 survey](https://pallas-data.ca/2026/08/25/pallas-toronto-poll-chow-50-bradford-39-alexander-8/) reported rounded decided/leaning support of Chow 50%, Bradford 39%, and Alexander 8% (808 Toronto eligible voters; released August 25, with an August 24 dateline). These are dated poll observations, not September 9 estimates or election-day forecasts. [Liaison's August 14–16 survey](https://press.liaisonstrategies.ca/toronto-chow-49-bradford-38-voters-want-balance-with-ford/) independently reported 49%, 38%, and 11%; it also found that 48% were unfamiliar with Alexander. That establishes different starting positions and familiarity, not different causal endorsement effects.

Our interpretation: Bradford already occupies the stronger challenger position in those polls. Alexander would need a much larger change to become the strongest challenger, and a gain for him could be accompanied by losses for Bradford. Conversely, a Bradford gain could come mostly from Alexander, from Chow, or from other sources. The 2023 aggregate polls do not identify which individuals switched and cannot supply those 2026 transfer rates. Tory's own former vote share or hypothetical candidacy support is not a transferable bloc.

Even the scale of extrapolation is unidentified. Forum's descriptive 13% → 20% movement can be represented as +7 percentage points or as an odds multiplier of about 1.67. Applying the fixed-point change to the rounded Pallas starting shares gives Bradford 46% or Alexander 15%; applying the same odds multiplier against all other choices gives 51.7% or 12.7%, respectively. These are alternative mathematical translations of the same historical association, not competing estimates of what Tory would cause. This is a reason to expose assumptions rather than conceal them inside a fitted coefficient.

### A concrete, reviewable first analysis

Use two recipient branches, Tory → Bradford and Tory → Alexander, with an unchanged reference for each. Define the quantity being varied as **additional net election-day percentage points for the recipient relative to the reference**, rather than a percent increase in existing support. Initially show -3, 0, +3, +6, and +9 points as illustrative settings. Using the same grid permits comparison; it does not assert equal effects for the two recipients. Positive settings span attenuated movement through roughly the first post-event increases in 2023; the negative and zero settings acknowledge that help is not established for either new recipient. These are analyst-selected stress settings, **not a confidence interval, a posterior, an empirical lower/upper bound, or equally likely outcomes**. The later 2023 surge can appear as a separate, explicitly labeled full-consolidation illustration, without being called Tory's effect or a maximum possible effect.

For every nonzero setting, state where the votes move. At minimum compare transfers from Chow, from the other named challenger, and a mixture. A more general tool can vary the fraction of each donor's support transferred. Any reverse effect must likewise have explicit recipients for the lost support. Do not assume all rival losses are proportional merely because that is convenient to code.

For a positive gain Δ to recipient k on an election-day share vector p, one possible representation is:

```text
p'[k] = p[k] + Δ
p'[j] = p[j] - w[j] × Δ   for every donor j
sum(w[j]) = 1; w[j] >= 0; w[j] × Δ <= p[j]
```

Here shares and Δ use the same units, and the last constraint applies to every draw, not just the average. Prefer transfer fractions when necessary to keep shares feasible; a fixed +9 points entirely from an 8-point donor is impossible. Never silently clip negative shares and renormalize, because that changes the stated scenario. Undecided respondents and nonvoters are not valid-vote candidates: resolving undecided choices or changing turnout requires a separate denominator-aware module and additional assumptions, not treating the Poll Residual as a donor candidate.

The donor matters as much as the gain. The following **arithmetic illustrations**, using Pallas's rounded 50/39/8 starting shares, hold the remaining support fixed and assume no other change:

| Assumed six-point transfer | Chow | Bradford | Alexander | Implication for the leading challenger |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Reference | 50 | 39 | 8 | Bradford trails by 11 |
| Chow → Bradford | 44 | 45 | 8 | Bradford leads by 1 |
| Alexander → Bradford | 50 | 45 | 2 | Bradford trails by 5 |
| Chow → Alexander | 44 | 39 | 14 | Bradford still leads Alexander; trails Chow by 5 |
| Bradford → Alexander | 50 | 33 | 14 | Bradford still leads Alexander; trails Chow by 17 |

These rows are author calculations, not polls asking about an endorsement, and do not assign win probabilities. They show why "Tory helps a challenger" does not determine whether Chow becomes easier to beat. Under this fixed-electorate arithmetic, closing Bradford's 11-point gap requires a 5.5-point transfer entirely from Chow, or an 11-point gain if Chow loses nothing. Alexander starts 42 points behind Chow: even a 21-point transfer entirely from Chow would only tie Chow at 29%, while Bradford would remain at 39%. Catching Chow and becoming the leading challenger are separate requirements. None of these thresholds is a probability statement about the eventual election.

### Timing, uncertainty, and the existing model

Timing must be an input. A five-days-before-election example cannot establish the persistence of an endorsement made weeks earlier. If Δ is defined directly as an election-day effect, persistence is already part of the assumed Δ. If instead the scenario starts with an immediate poll response, separately expose persistence and the fraction of the electorate still able to change its vote; those quantities are not learned from the 2023 before/after difference. Do not mechanically multiply a poll's observed change by the share yet to vote without checking whether its respondent denominator already includes people who voted early.

The current [Mayoral Endpoint Bridge](../../backend/model/mayoral_endpoint.py) combines comparable poll readings and produces full-ballot draws; it does not estimate a latent daily path or an endorsement response. [The forecast feed](../../backend/model/mayoral_forecast_feed.py) reduces the same joint draws to margins and win probabilities. A future sensitivity tool could transform paired baseline draws with explicit transfers and re-run that reducer, preserving the relationship between candidates rather than adding independent candidate bonuses. Reusing the draws and reducer does **not** make the transformed distribution historically calibrated.

The endpoint reference is also not an identified "world in which Tory never endorses anyone": ordinary future campaign movement is already part of forecast uncertainty. Label the contrast "additional assumed shift relative to the reference forecast." A causal no-endorsement counterfactual would need a richer design. Once real post-event polling arrives, it already contains any response; an overlay added in full on top risks counting the same movement twice. Explicit event updating would require a new component and validation, rather than leaving a permanent bonus in place.

For a first version, show support and margin sensitivity, and a break-even threshold, without estimated endorsement-conditioned win probabilities. If probabilities are explored internally later, distinguish baseline election uncertainty, assumed response/transfer/persistence uncertainty, and Monte Carlo error. The narrow numerical sampling intervals produced by `forecast_quantities` cannot describe uncertainty in Tory's causal effect. Existing publication gates do not confer qualification on a new scenario mechanism.

This fits the repository's present boundaries: [ADR 0007](../adr/0007-admit-political-signals-only-by-validation.md) requires reconstruction and performance on whole held-out election cycles for predictive admission; [ADR 0008](../adr/0008-treat-endorsements-as-updatable-events.md) specifies uncertain, updatable event responses if admitted. [ADR 0040](../adr/0040-exclude-by-elections-from-the-qualification-gate.md) treats the 2023 by-election as report-only for the qualification decision on a regular-election forecast. Multiple polls around one endorsement do not become multiple independently validated endorsements. An editorial sensitivity analysis is possible under these boundaries; one observed surge does not qualify an operational predictive feature.

### Evidence that would improve the analysis

1. Reconstruct the whole 2023 sequence with fixed pre-event windows and compare a flat baseline with pre-event trend extrapolations, always within pollster and denominator. A house-effect-aware time-series fit could summarize how unusual the movement was, but its post-event coefficient would still describe a bundled late-campaign change. Rival candidates are affected by vote transfers and are not untreated controls. Same-day Star and Tory endorsements cannot be separately estimated merely by adding two coincident indicators.
2. Obtain timestamped respondent-level polls or an existing pre/post panel if the pollster can supply them with suitable provenance. Second choices, prior vote intention, endorsement awareness, voting timing, and persistent respondent identity would help distinguish transitions from changing sample composition. Aware-versus-unaware comparisons are still observational; a panel alone does not remove common campaign shocks or establish causality. Ward-level Tory-2022/Bailão-2023 correlations likewise do not isolate endorsement effects.
3. A representative Toronto survey experiment could randomly assign a control condition, a clearly hypothetical Tory→Bradford endorsement, or a clearly hypothetical Tory→Alexander endorsement, and measure the same ballot question. Keep the ballot and accompanying information otherwise identical and account for the precision lost by splitting the sample. This could estimate the immediate effect of the specified hypothetical information on stated preferences, not campaign-wide reach, durable persuasion, turnout, or election results. Merely asking whether Tory would make someone "more likely" to vote for a candidate is not a net vote-share estimate.
4. For predictive inclusion, reconstruct a predeclared class of comparable endorsements including null or adverse cases, retain source times and fieldwork exposure, and evaluate complete held-out election cycles. Do not select only famous endorsements followed by surges. Use 2023 as a relevant case study and stress case while respecting its different election type.
